Featured case study

Crop Risk مخاطر المحاصيل

A machine-learning tool that estimates an Egyptian crop's next-season risk from its own yield history, the water it receives, its soil and its growth stage.

Open live app API docs Rules & assumptions
77crops with ≥ 4 years of data
35years of data, 1990–2024
2,645yield records
4forecast models to compare

The problem

Yield forecasts alone don't say how much a season can go wrong. Crop Risk turns a crop's own history into a risk estimate and adjusts it for the conditions a farmer can actually influence or observe, such as how much of the crop's water requirement is supplied.

How it works

  1. Data FAOSTAT crop yields for Egypt (QCL, 1990–2024), in kg/ha.
  2. Forecast The user picks a model: naive baseline, polynomial, linear regression or random forest. Models are compared by out-of-sample error on the held-out years 2016–2024.
  3. Adjust Water supplied (as a % of requirement), soil type and growth stage feed into the risk estimate. Stage sensitivity coefficients (Ky) are sourced for wheat only.
  4. Report A risk result for the next season, with its source explained. A separate model estimates the probability of a yield drop of 10% or more.

Inputs

  • Crop
  • Soil: loam, clay, sandy, saline
  • Water received (%)
  • Growth stage: vegetative → ripening
  • Forecast model

Key features

  • Fully bilingual interface, English and Arabic.
  • Documented API with interactive Swagger docs.
  • Machine-readable rules endpoint listing every assumption.
  • Deployed live on Modal.

Known limitations, stated openly

The project publishes its own caveats, and so do I:

  • The drop-probability model is a weak signal, with AUC around 0.6 (0.5 is random).
  • About 32% of the FAOSTAT values are estimated or imputed, not official.
  • Soil points, non-wheat Ky values and the 15 / 35 risk bands are assumptions or presentation choices, not validated cut-offs.

Built with: Python · regression & random-forest models · FAOSTAT data · REST API · Modal
